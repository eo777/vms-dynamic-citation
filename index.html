GOVERNED SELF-CITATION

Overview

Timm's self-citation generator guided by preferences for component patterns. Compare the generated texts against the manuscript, with page replay for the original featured runs.

What this is

  • The generator. Torsten Timm's program writes Voynich-like text by copying a word from the lines above and changing it slightly: replace a glyph, add or drop a prefix, join two words, split one.
  • The model. A fitted positional model gives, for each of 50 bifolia in the proposed order, the expected share of 1,324 spelling constructions (prefixes, suffixes, interior spans, patterns).
  • The governor. Three hooks let the model steer two of Timm's draws: which word is copied, and whether a change is kept. The steering signal is the gap between the model's target and the text written so far.
  • The innovator. Where the text runs short of new spellings, the governor replaces a repeated proposal by an edit or a join of it, chosen by the same signal.

Caveats

  • Everything is conditional on the proposed order of the bifolia and on the fitted model; neither is tested here.
  • The model supplies expected component shares. The comparison uses manuscript text in Path 1 order for sequential statistics; generated text is evaluated in matching token-budget intervals. The comparison labels do not change the generator's component-based preference trajectory.
  • Generated pages follow the manuscript's page and line geometry along the route (characters per line, paragraph ends, page ends); the words are the generator's, the layout is the manuscript's.
  • The governed run is one realisation of a stochastic process; the seed ranges show how much the summary statistics move.
